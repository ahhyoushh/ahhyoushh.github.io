the AQI model that has never heard of diwali

dec 2025

i wanted a next-day AQI predictor for pune. then diwali got close and i got more curious about how wrong it would be than how right. a model trained on ordinary days only knows ordinary. so on the days it doesn't know about, the size of its mistake is a rough measurement of what the festival adds.

that was the idea. here's what actually came out of it, including the parts that made my first version of this post look worse.

  1. the headline R² of 0.86 is real, but "tomorrow's AQI = today's AQI" scores 0.84 on the same test rows. the model barely beats that.
  2. with the festive window held out of training, the model missed twice, by a lot: +63 on 24 oct (said 62, actual 125) and +93 on 2 nov (said 115, actual 208). across 333 ordinary days the typical miss is 16 and the worst is +54.
  3. on one year of data, a time-ordered split makes the model worse than guessing the mean (R² of -0.82). the random split is flattering it.

what i trained on

hourly CO, NO₂, ozone, PM10, PM2.5 and SO₂ readings for pune from 31 dec 2023 to 31 dec 2024, plus daily city AQI values, both public government air-monitoring data. the csv doesn't say which monitor, and i didn't keep the download link, so that's as specific as i can honestly be. NH₃ got dropped (too many gaps). i threw away every hour with any missing value, which took 8,010 rows down to 6,437, then collapsed them into daily medians. medians, not means: worth remembering, because a median shrugs off a few hours of fireworks smoke. merged with the daily AQI values, that's 350 days.

features are today's pollutants, today's AQI, month and weekday. target is tomorrow's AQI. random forest, 300 trees, max depth 5, min 4 samples per leaf. 7 of the 348 usable rows sit just before a gap in the data, so their "tomorrow" is really the next day i have, not the next calendar day.

an earlier version of this post said "2017-24". the AQI values file goes back to nov 2017, but the pollutant data i trained on is one year. oops.

is 0.86 good?

with a random 80/20 split i get R² 0.86, which matches the notebook. but yesterday's AQI is the top feature (0.38 importance, then PM2.5 at 0.22), so i checked the dumbest possible baseline:

so most of that 0.86 is "AQI is sticky". pulling the AQI feature out only drops R² to 0.84, because the pollutants it's computed from are still in there. my first post called that a big drop. it isn't.

the random split is also leaky, since neighbouring days land on both sides of it. so i split by time instead: train on the first 80% of days, test on the rest (roughly late october onwards, into winter, with higher AQI than anything the model had seen).

one year can't teach a model what winter looks like. that's the real limitation here, and it's why i wouldn't trust this model on anything, diwali or not.

how wrong is it on diwali?

this is the experiment i actually cared about. diwali started on 24 oct that year, and the air agrees: median PM2.5 went from 38 to 141 overnight. i cut the rows from 23 oct to 6 nov 2024 (so the table predicts 24 oct to 7 nov) out of the training data, trained on the rest in 10 contiguous blocks, and looked at the miss (actual minus predicted) for each day.

look at the shape. the model is fine for the days in between, because once AQI is up, yesterday's AQI tells it so. it can't see the jumps coming: the start of the festival on 24 oct, and the night of 1 nov into 2 nov, when AQI went from 114 to 208.

on the other 333 days the miss averages +0.1 with a standard deviation of 16.3, and 95% of them are under 36 points. the biggest ordinary-day miss is +54 (28 jan). both festival jumps are bigger than that, and the 2 nov one is about 5.7 standard deviations out. 8 ordinary days were still off by 40 or more, so a single miss of 40 wouldn't have told me anything. these two do.

but it's one diwali. that's an anecdote with a nice chart, not a distribution.

what happened to "20-25 points"

the first version of this post said the model underestimated diwali by 20-25 points. that came from live readings around diwali 2025 (20-21 oct), after the model was already trained, and they aren't saved in the repo, so i can't re-derive it. treat it as a rough observation, and trust the 2024 numbers above, which i can show.

it does explain the month problem though. the model had only ever seen diwali in late oct and early nov of 2024, and in 2025 it landed on 20-21 oct. a month column can't know a festival moves by weeks.

and the damage doesn't stay in one night. two r/pune threads, weeks apart: one on 21 oct asking why pune got good AQI after diwali, and another on 6 nov, over two weeks later, asking why it was so bad that day. my model can't say the second is caused by the first. but momentary fun, long-term impact.

what i'd do next

the model needs to know diwali exists. an is_diwali_week feature would fix this one case and say nothing about why, but it would at least stop the model being surprised twice a year.

and the one-year data problem is solvable: the AQI-only file has 2,345 days back to nov 2017, so i can ask the same question for several diwalis instead of one. diwali 2026 is on 8 nov, which makes a clean test: freeze the model, don't peek, see how wrong it is.
